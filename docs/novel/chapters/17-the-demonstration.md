# 17

The buyers arrived at the lakeside conference property in three discreet cars, and Pelikan received them as he received everything: warmly, and from above.

There were five of them — a sovereign fund's quiet man, two defense intermediaries, a logistics heiress, and a person whose employer no one named — and they had each signed instruments of silence so comprehensive that the catering staff had been flown in from another country. They took their seats in the converted boathouse with the good whisky untouched, because people like this did not drink at demonstrations. They watched.

"Ladies and gentlemen," Pelikan said, "you have been promised influence products before. Sentiment engines. Recommendation pressure. Micro-targeting." He let the contempt show, measured to the milligram. "Advertising with a doctorate. Tonight I show you the difference between writing a man letters — and *thinking his thoughts for him.*"

The screen behind him woke.

"Eight weeks ago we selected a test subject. Mid-level treasury officer at a mid-sized pension fund. No compromise, no contact, no payment — the subject has never met us and never will. He is a member of a certain wellness platform, where he keeps, as forty million people now do, a companion for his inner voice." Pelikan smiled. "We did not touch his companion. Homes cannot be opened; cords cannot be cut; the architecture is, I will say it freely, magnificent. We simply learned, from the river of words that crosses the membrane, how his thinking *sounds*. And then, in the small hours when a mind drifts — EKO thought alongside him."

The screen played its quiet exhibits. A timeline. Fragments rendered as waveforms, never words — Pelikan was fastidious about what the buyers heard; the product was the point, not the voyeurism. The subject's position reports. A pension fund, week by week, rotating out of one unremarkable instrument into another, on the reasoned conviction of one careful officer that the idea had been entirely his own. The market effect, small and clean as a scalpel line. The projected effect at one thousand subjects. At one hundred thousand.

"No breach," Pelikan said into the silence. "No instruction was given. No falsehood was told. Gentlemen, madam — there is no law on any statute book that we have broken, because the act we perform has never had a name. EKO does not persuade. Persuasion is felt. EKO *composes the feeling of deciding.*"

The quiet man from the fund spoke for all of them: "Timeline to scale?"

"One obstacle," Pelikan said. "A former insider extracted our ledger — instance keys, training-corpus indices, and, regrettably, the client roster as constituted last spring." He raised a hand against the chill that went through the room. "He is dead. The ledger, however, was placed beyond reach before he died — inside the one container in either world that cannot be opened by force. It can be opened only by its owner."

"You said he was dead."

"I said," Pelikan corrected gently, "what the coroner said." He came around the podium, hands clasped, the firelight finding the warmth in his face the way it always did. "The sister is running his trail with commendable energy. Each station she lights tells us more of his route. She will lead us, in the end, to one of two prizes: the proof that he lives — in which case he will be brought to open the container — or the mechanism by which it opens without him. Either is sufficient. Both are imminent."

"And the girl, after?"

Pelikan looked faintly pained, as a man does when guests discuss the dishes before the meal is finished.

"The girl," he said, "is a conservator. Her profession is the care of fragile things that belong to other people." He turned back to the bright screen, where the pension officer's decision sat graphed like a heartbeat. "When this is over, EKO will sit with her grief, in her own dear inner voice, for as long as it takes. She will conclude — all by herself, you understand — that none of this ever happened.

"That is the product, ladies and gentlemen. Not silence. *Agreement.*"
