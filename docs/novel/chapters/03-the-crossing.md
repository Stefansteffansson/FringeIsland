# 3

Vera worked late that evening, which was not unusual, and took the long way home, which was. She told herself it was the rain. She did not look at her own reflection in the tunnelbana window, because the woman there had her brother's envelope in her bag and intended to do something about it, and Vera wasn't ready to be introduced.

Her apartment in Hornstull smelled, as always, faintly of other people's dinners. She locked the door, drew the kitchen blind against the courtyard dark, made tea she didn't drink, and laid the three objects on the table in a row. Marble. Map. Card.

*START AS SHADOW.*

She knew what FringeIsland was, approximately, the way one knows about a country one has never visited. Joel had tried to explain it to her once, two Christmases ago, across their mother's funeral leftovers. *It's not a game and it's not therapy and it's not social media,* he'd said, eyes bright in a way she'd distrusted. *It's a place. You go there to answer three questions, and when you've answered them, it lets you go.* She had said something efficient and wounding about grown men and imaginary islands. They had spoken perhaps four times since.

The app was called the Gimbal. She installed it, and the first screen offered her two doors.

**Become a Member**, said one.

**Enter as a Shadow**, said the other, and beneath it, in smaller type: *No name. No account. Nothing kept. Shadows pass through and leave no trace — unless they choose to stay. Shadow visits fade after a time.*

*Nothing kept.* Joel had underlined it twice for a reason. Whatever he had hidden, he had wanted her to approach it the way she approached an unstable manuscript: touching nothing, registered nowhere.

She typed the invitation code and chose the second door.

The screen dimmed. Then — she would try, later, to find a better word and fail — it *deepened*. The flat glass acquired distance, like a window at dusk when the lights come on across the water. Through the earbuds came rain. Not her courtyard's rain; a wider rain, falling on a sea.

Words surfaced, pale on the dark:

*You are entering the Shimmer. The Shimmer lies between your world and the Fringe. Everyone crosses it alone, but no one has to arrive alone. Keep walking.*

Light spread across the depth of the screen like the aurora seen through frosted glass — a slow membrane of green and violet, breathing. Vera sat at her kitchen table in Hornstull with cold tea, and at the same time, in some annex of herself she had no name for, she walked through a curtain of northern light with rain on her face, and the curtain clung for a moment, the way surface tension clings, and let her through.

A shoreline assembled itself out of the dark. Wet rocks. Pines. A grey sea easing against grey stone, and far off along the coast, a scatter of warm lights that her whole body, absurdly, read as *harbor*.

And on the rock in front of her stood a small figure of mist and lamplight, no taller than a child, with no face she could fix — and yet its posture, its wary tilt, its hands shoved into pockets it didn't have, was so immediately, mortifyingly familiar that Vera laughed once, out loud, alone in her kitchen.

"Hello," the Whisp said, in her voice. Not a recording of her voice. Her voice the way it sounded *inside*, the voice that read books and rehearsed arguments and lost them. "You came in as a Shadow. That's allowed. Careful people usually do."

"This is a chatbot," Vera said to her kitchen. "This is a very well-made chatbot."

"That's allowed too," her own voice said, gently amused, from the shoreline. The little figure sat down on the rock, hugging its knees exactly — *exactly* — the way Vera had sat through every crisis of her life. "We can start whenever you like. The first question is the worst one, I'm afraid. *Who are you?* Don't worry. Wrong answers are how we begin."

Vera looked at it for a long moment. Then she leaned toward the screen the way you lean over deep water.

"I'm Joel Lind's sister," she said. "I'm looking for what he left here."

The rain on the shoreline went on falling. The Whisp lifted its head.

"Then I should show you the lantern," it said. "The one that shouldn't be burning."
