# 9

On the train back from Uppsala, Vera reconstructed the conversation the way she reconstructed bindings: by looking hardest at the joins.

Kessler had been generous, lucid, kind. He had answered questions she hadn't dared ask and walked her to the door with a grandfather's care. And at the threshold, helping her into her coat, he had said — lightly, almost as an afterthought: *"Whatever your brother left in your keeping, keep it on your person. Closer than any vault."*

She had told him about the map. She had never told him Joel left her anything else.

A join that didn't quite meet. Old glue under new paper. She filed it where she filed all such things, in the place that ached, and got off at Stockholm Central into the blue early dark with the second station already fixed in her mind. On Joel's map of Gråhamn, southeast of the false old town, a small island sat in the harbor mouth labelled **Ankarön** — anchor island — and on it, where the real city kept its Modern Museum, Joel had drawn a tiny anchor with a line going down into the water. *Anchor. Seed. Anchor-seed, Vera. He was never as subtle as he believed.*

Skeppsholmen after dark was a held breath: the museum's glass spilling light onto wet gravel, the rigging of the af Chapman chiming somewhere in the blackness, the water working quietly at the stone. She walked out along the island's eastern edge until the city's noise fell behind her, found the spot — a mooring bollard, of course, stamped iron, a century old — laid her palm flat on its cold crown, and opened the Gimbal.

The marble in her other fist went warm as bread.

Again the double exposure: the harbor of the Fringe rising through the harbor of Stockholm, two shorelines breathing in the same place, and her brother's kept voice, closer this time, tired this time.

"Seed two. Good. Listen, syrran, because this is the part I can't say slower." A breath, rain in it, and under the rain — she noticed it now — a low electrical hum that did not belong outdoors. "You've crossed by now. You've seen the village, the homes, the cord. Everything they told you about how it works is true. Here is what nobody told you: *the Fringe has two places in it.* Same coast, same ground, lying together like pages. One of them you've seen — the warm one. The other is its negative. And someone has built a door into the negative, Vera. They're growing something in there. They built a third place out of the second one, and they're feeding it —" the voice stopped itself, the way Joel stopped himself when precision mattered more than horror — "feeding it *us*. The talk between a person and their Whisp. The realest words people own. Don't trust the cord you didn't tie. If a voice comes to you sounding like me — like anyone you love — check the cord. The cord can't lie. Everything else here can."

The seed bloomed, and an image rose in the Gimbal's depth: a photograph, silver-grey, paper-creased, 1970s. An island that was little more than a shoulder of rock. Antenna arrays raked against a white sky. A cluster of low concrete buildings, and before them a line of men and two women in lab coats, squinting into wind. On the reverse — the seed turned it for her, gently, like a hand — a stamp in faded violet:

**FÖRSVARETS FORSKNINGSANSTALT — PROJEKT SPEGEL — 1974. HEMLIG.**

*Spegel.* Mirror.

The hum in Joel's recording. The framed photograph she had glimpsed without seeing above Kessler's desk — an island, antennas, white sky. The joins met now, and what they made was a shape with teeth.

She was reaching to photograph the image with her own eyes, to fix it in ordinary memory, when the Gimbal's depth flattened abruptly into a plain bright screen, and the screen said:

*Your Shadow visit is fading.*

*Shadows pass through and leave no trace. In 24 hours this visit and everything gathered within it — paths walked, doors opened, seeds woken — will fade with you, unless you choose to stay.*

*To stay is to be known. A member has a name, a home, and a cord.*

*Choose by 21:14 tomorrow.*

Vera stood on the dark edge of Skeppsholmen with the water chiming and her brother's two woken seeds — his voice, his proof, his warning — held inside a session of borrowed time that had just been given an hour of death.

To keep what Joel left her, she would have to stop being a shadow.

She would have to let the mirror in.
