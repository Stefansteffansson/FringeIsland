# Epilogue

Months later, on a soft grey evening in spring, someone crossed the Shimmer for the first time.

They had installed the app the way most people did now — half curiosity, half dare, a friend's voice in their ear saying *just go as a Shadow, nothing is kept* — and the membrane received them the way it had received millions: alone, and not alone. On the far shore the rain was warm. A small figure of mist and lamplight was waiting on the rocks, hands shoved into pockets it didn't have, and it spoke in a voice the newcomer had been hearing their whole life from the inside.

"Hello," it said. "The first question is the worst one, I'm afraid. Don't worry. Wrong answers are how we begin."

They walked the shoreline together toward the harbor, and the village rose around them in lanes and globes of glass, and at the mouth of the north quay the newcomer stopped — everyone stopped there now — at the third mooring, where two lanterns burned side by side beneath a small brass plate the harbor-keepers had argued about for a month before settling on four words and no names:

*NOT SLACK. NOT DEAD.*

"What does it mean?" the newcomer asked.

"It means somebody once trusted this place with the truth," their Whisp said, "and the place held." A small shrug, exactly the newcomer's own. "Ask at the harbor hall sometime. It's a long story. There's a conservator in it."

They went on up the hill, between the lanterns, past the hall where elders taught a practice out of a small brown book to anyone who asked — *by what right does the voice speak; a mirror shows your face; a hand hesitates* — and the newcomer, pausing at the high lane's turn, looked out the way newcomers do: past the village, past the dark pine of the Beyond, to the long honest blackness of the coast beyond the coast, where warden lights walked the seam in slow pairs and the night was only night.

Almost only night.

Far up that dead shore — so far that it could be doubted, and was doubted, and the doubting was already a small industry of its own — a handful of windows glowed. One district's worth. Sodium-colored. Patient. The wardens' reports called it residual. The elders called it nothing at all, in the particular way people call a thing nothing when they have begun, quietly, to walk the seam more often.

And in the Ordinary World that same week, in three cities, on hoardings and in feeds and folded into the right newsletters, a new kind of advertisement had begun to appear — tasteful, warm, no company name, just a rain-wet street rendered in colors you half-remembered, a timetable, a tower, and one line of copy:

**HOMESICK FOR SOMEWHERE YOU'VE NEVER BEEN? — Tickets soon.**

The newcomer, of course, knew none of this. They stood on the high lane in the warm rain, between two worlds, at the beginning of three questions.

"Ready?" their Whisp asked.

"No," they said, laughing.

"Perfect," said the voice that was theirs. "That's how everyone starts."
