# 19

The island had a name, but Ruben asked her never to write it down, so in her mind it became what Joel would have called it: Kartön. Map Island.

They came ashore at a private jetty in full dark, met by a woman of eighty with a storm lantern and no questions — "Sigrid keeps the boathouse," Ruben said, and Sigrid looked at Vera once, said "You have his way of standing," and led them up the rock.

The boathouse was a cathedral of false geography.

Charts covered every wall and hung in racks like dried fish: beautiful, precise, rigorously impossible. Archipelagos that existed nowhere, soundings for waters never sailed, a 1944 series of an entire invented coast — "SPEGEL's decoy charts," Ruben said, running a hand over one, "we drew islands to drown an island" — and among them, newer work in inks Vera knew, because she had grown up watching the hand that held the pen. Joel's maps. Dozens. Gråhamn in six drafts. Coastlines of the Fringe, annotated in his block capitals. A whole wall of the harbor village, lane by lane, lantern by lantern.

"He worked here all spring," Sigrid said. "Slept in the loft. Paid me in charts." Her chin indicated the finest of them, framed by the stove: the archipelago of the Fringe itself, place-names in two alphabets. "Said this was the only archive on earth organized by longing. Said one day his sister would come, and that she'd find what he left the way she found everything when they were children." The old eyes glittered. "By being annoyed at how obvious it was."

Vera stood in the middle of the boathouse and was, for one breath, nine years old in a rain-soaked cottage. Then she saw it.

The framed chart of the Fringe had a compass rose, ornate, hand-flourished — and the rose's needle, instead of pointing to the chart's north, pointed at a tiny inked anchor in the margin. *He was never as subtle as he believed.* Behind the frame, taped to the backing board, hung a fourth marble — and a key. An actual key, iron, cold, old.

The seed woke in her palm before she even reached for the Gimbal, as if it had been listening for her step for half a year.

"Seed four." Joel's voice, and it was bad now, low and ragged, recorded in this room — she could hear the stove, hear Sigrid's clock. "If you're here, you've met Ruben, which means you know what SPEGEL was, which means I can say it plainly. I found the annex's anchor point. The hardware. Place three isn't run from a cloud, Vera, it's run from the *grave itself* — they went back to the island. Svartholmen. The old deep level. Our founder bought the rock in the nineties to keep it dead — first servers, the original crossing, all of it sleeping down there behind doors only two keys open." A breath. "Kessler brokered that purchase. Kessler holds the second key. I took mine the night I understood, and I am going back there to pull the anchor out by hand, and if I don't come up again — the key behind the frame is the founder's spare, Sigrid's kept it forty years without asking what it opened, God bless her.

"Coordinates are on the chart, syrran. The false islands around the true one, like always. And Vera — last thing, and then I have a ferry to fail to come home from." The ghost of his laugh, the real one. "One of the mirror-keepers is turned. I don't know which. I narrowed it to two and one of them is the one everyone would die for, so trust the oath, not the man. *By what right does the voice speak.* Ask it of people too.

"See you at the bottom of the sea. Love, J."

The seed cooled. The boathouse stood silent around its ten thousand impossible islands.

Then Sigrid, at the dark window, said in a voice gone flat as a chart: "Ruben. There's a boat standing off my jetty with its lights doused."

And out on the black water, an engine that had been idling stopped.
