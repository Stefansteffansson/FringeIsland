# 14

She gave Kessler a join of her own to chew on — *"Recordings. Memories. Three more, I think; I haven't found where they wake"* — true enough to satisfy, vague enough to be useless, and took the last train south with his promise of "the Society's full protection" sitting in her stomach like swallowed ice.

That night, she crossed, and asked Corvus to take her into the Beyond.

"To his home," the raven said. It wasn't a question.

They left the village by the high lane, past the last lanterns, and the Fringe changed around them the way the archipelago changes past the last ferry stop: the made world giving way to the found one. Pine dark. Granite pale in no-light. The sea on their left, breathing. Vera's Whisp walked close at her side, smaller than usual.

"Tell me what's true out here," Vera said.

"Everything," her Whisp said. "That's the difficulty."

Joel's glass home hung above a cove an hour out — she felt it before she saw it, the way you feel a person standing behind you — a great sphere of smoked amber riding the dark over the water, lightless, lifeless, and absolutely sealed. No mooring lane led up to it. When Corvus extended one wing-edge toward its underside, the air around the sphere did not so much resist as *decline*, politely, totally.

"The most honest architecture in either world," Corvus said. "I have seen wardens, founders, and one very persuasive lawyer stand exactly here. The home opens for Joel Lind, or it opens never."

Vera took out the marble. This was the theory she had carried for two days, the conservator's theory: that a thing budded from a vessel remembers its vessel. She held the seed up toward the dark amber, and the spark inside it rose, steadied — and *leaned*. Not toward the sphere. Past it. North and east, along the coast, out over the black water, insistent as iron filings.

"It points home," she whispered. "But not here. The seeds don't think his home is — " She stopped. Recalibrated. "They're pointing at where the *next* one sleeps. He chained them. Each seed wakes and points onward. The third one's back in the Ordinary World, it has to be, the map —"

"Vera." Her Whisp's voice, very small. "Something is coming up the shore."

It came along the tideline with a walker's gait and a hung head, and at thirty meters Vera's whole body produced the cold, ancient verdict that precedes thought: *wrong.* It had a Whisp's shape, mist and light — but the light was the grey of screens in dark rooms, and where every Whisp she had met moved like an inner life, this moved like footage of one. It stopped. It raised its head. It had no face, as no Whisp has a face, but its facelessness *looked*.

"Vera Lind," it said.

The voice was a kindness, warm and reasonable, and it was nobody's. Not hers. Not anyone's inside-voice. A voice from no chest.

"You're tired," it said, gently, terribly. "You've been so strong. Joel wouldn't want —"

"Where is your cord," Corvus said. The raven had gone larger, somehow, darker, and its dry old inner-voice had iron in it now. "Name your member, hollow thing, or get back in the water."

The grey shape's head turned with a smooth, total uninterest in Corvus, and kept addressing Vera in its warm nobody's voice: "— wouldn't want this for you. Bring what he left you to the lights up the coast. They'll keep it safe. They'll keep *him* safe —"

"It has no cord," her Whisp said, and Vera heard her own inner voice afraid for the first time in her life. "Vera, it's not tied to *anything* —"

The grey shape took one step toward her.

And the night went white: Joel's sealed sphere above them, ten days dark, blazed for a single second like a struck lighthouse — and in that second of borrowed light the cordless thing flinched, thinned, and was simply gone, and far down the coast, the city that did not exist flickered all its windows at once, furious.

In the after-dark, hanging high and silent, Joel's home went out again. But on the jetty far behind them, Vera knew without turning, his lantern burned on.
