# 12

She took the question to the only counsel she trusted: a raven made of someone's inner dark, on a jetty in a world the police could not subpoena.

"Kessler," Corvus said, and the word came out of the wing-shadow slowly, the way one lifts a stone to see what lives under it. "Yes. I know of him. The Society's great diplomat. The man who argued for initiating the founder instead of burying him." The raven-shape tilted. "What did he get wrong?"

"He knew things I didn't tell him." She laid it out: the slip about *keeping it on your person*. The phone number she'd never given. And the photograph above the desk — island, antennas, white sky — twin to the one Joel's seed had shown her, stamped SPEGEL, stamped *secret*.

Corvus was silent for a long time. Water knocked under the planks.

"Now I will disappoint you," it said at last, "by being fair. Hear the innocent reading. The Society has watched SPEGEL for fifty years — of course its senior keeper owns the photograph; it is the file he guards. Edvard called him after the library, frightened, an old colleague — there is your phone number, passed on with the best intentions of a man who then fell down a staircase. And *keep it on your person* is what any custodian of hidden things says by reflex." The raven head turned to her. "Every fact you have fits a guardian as well as it fits a wolf, Vera Lind. That is not an accident. Men like Kessler live their whole lives in clothing that fits both."

"Then what do I do?"

"You go to Uppsala," Corvus said, "exactly as summoned. You let him be warm. You eat his biscuits. And you treat every word he says as true — because the most dangerous men tell the truth in bulk, and lie only at the join." A dry rasp that was the raven's laugh. "You're a conservator. Look for the join."

The Ferryman received his new instructions that evening, move 19 of the Queen's Gambit, and read them twice, because for the first time in this engagement they were sloppy.

*Subject travels to Uppsala tonight. Security posture only — she is to arrive untouched and unafraid. Stand down all visible coverage.*

That was not the sloppy part. The sloppy part was the metadata, which the client could not see and the Ferryman could not help seeing, because he had been a careful man for nineteen years: the move had been posted from a network whose routing he had quietly fingerprinted weeks ago, out of professional hygiene. Every prior move had come through anonymizing layers in three countries.

This one had been posted from a residential fiber line in Uppsala.

The Ferryman sat with his cooling kettle and his graph paper and did not write anything down, which was, for him, the equivalent of another man pacing all night. The client was the kind of man who never licked an envelope. Tonight he had posted from his own house — which meant tonight the client was excited, and excitement in careful men meant the schedule had moved, and *moved schedules* were where people like the Ferryman were spent like small coins.

He thought, not for the first time, about the forty seconds of tape. The product that spoke in his own voice. He thought about what a man who owned such a product would eventually need from a contractor who had heard it.

Then he opened a drawer, took out a second notebook — one no client had ever paid for — and began, in his private notation, a new ledger.

At the top of the first page he wrote a single word. It was not a job number.

It was *PELIKAN*.
