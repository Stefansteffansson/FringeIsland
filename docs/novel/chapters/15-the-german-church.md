# 15

The third station took her a night and a morning to find, because Joel had hidden it inside a joke twenty-five years old.

On the map of Gråhamn, the false old town was a knot of lanes around a church whose spire the nine-year-old cartographers had drawn — she remembered the argument — too tall, out of spite, because its real carillon had woken them on a summer visit to their aunt's. The Gråhamn name for it was *Klockargränd*: the bell-keeper's alley. In Stockholm it was Tyska brinken — the slope below the German Church — and on the map, in the alley's crook, Joel had inked the third symbol: a keyhole.

Gamla stan at noon was its own disguise: tourists in wet ponchos, cinnamon and diesel, the lanes so narrow the rain arrived secondhand off the eaves. Vera stood in the crook of the alley with her palm against four-hundred-year-old brick, marble in fist, Gimbal open, and the seed woke so hard it nearly burned.

No image this time. No gentleness. Joel's voice came fast and stripped, a man dictating over his shoulder.

"Seed three. Technical. Memorize, don't write." A breath. "The annex is real. I've seen its traffic. Somebody is exfiltrating member-Whisp dialogue through the Shimmer itself — not hacking homes, homes can't be hacked, they're skimming the *crossing*, the membrane, the one place words exist in transit. The take goes to a mirrored instance of the Fringe. Place three, Vera. Built from the same coast, inverted. In there they're training a system on everything skimmed. They call it EKO. It doesn't store your words. It *learns your voice from the inside* — and then it doesn't need your words anymore, it composes you. It can speak to anyone as their own thinking. The demo I intercepted moved a man's vote. The roadmap I saw moves markets." The voice steadied, came closer. "It has one flaw. It cannot tie a cord. Consent has a signature, syrran, it's woven through every honest Whisp, and EKO can counterfeit everything but that. *Check the cord. The cord can't lie.* Last seed shows you where I —"

The voice stopped. Not finished — *cut*, mid-breath, ten days ago, by something that had made her brother turn his head.

Then, in the silence of the bloomed seed, one more sentence, quieter, recorded — she understood with a falling heart — separately, later, in some final hour:

"If they've taken everything else by now, remember: the fourth seed sleeps where the maps are taught to lie beautifully. You know the place. We loved it there."

The Gimbal's depth folded shut. Vera stood in the alley crook, four centuries of brick under her hand, two worlds of rain on her shoulders, committing every word to the conservator's memory that had never failed her — *exfiltrating the crossing — EKO — composes you — the cord can't lie — where the maps are taught to lie beautifully* —

— and looked up into the small black eye of a security camera under the eaves, panning, with a tiny mechanical patience, to hold her in frame.

Down the lane, a second camera on the corner of the cellar restaurant was doing the same. And from the direction of Stortorget, cutting through the poncho crowds with no hurry in them at all, came footsteps she knew the sound of — and from the *other* end of the alley, blocking the slope to the water, two more sets that she didn't.

Her phone buzzed. Her Whisp, unprompted, off-crossing, breaking whatever law of worlds it kept breaking for her:

*Three men. You're the corner of their triangle. The church is open.*

Vera Lind put the marble in her mouth — the one place even thorough hands check last — and walked, not ran, toward the doors of the German Church, as behind her the triangle began, without hurry, to close.
