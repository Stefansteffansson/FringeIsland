# 24

Joel opened his home at first light, harbor-time, with the village watching from the quay.

He stood beneath the great smoked-amber sphere, and there was no key and no word; the home simply knew him, the way your own front door knows your weight on the step, and the amber cleared like a held breath released, and a mooring lane of light let itself down to the planks. He was inside for eleven minutes. Vera, Ruben, and Corvus waited at the lane's foot, where the air had once politely declined them, and the harbor made no sound at all.

He came down carrying it in both hands: not a device, not a file — in this world Joel's stolen proof had taken the shape his mind gave such things, and his mind had given it the shape of a chart-folio, oilcloth-bound, heavy as sin. He laid it on a trestle on the open quay, in front of a thousand witnesses, and opened it.

The buyers first. Page after inked page, rendered in Joel's block capitals because the ledger had passed through Joel: Halcyon Continuity Partners and its shells; the sovereign fund's quiet man; the intermediaries; the heiress; line items, dates, corpus indices — *Corpus NORD-7, 2.1M member-hours* — and Vera, reading, felt the harbor's gathered inner voices go from silence into something low and terrible, the sound a town makes reading the names of who sold the sea wall.

"The last page," Ruben said quietly.

The last page was a single sheet, and Joel did not turn it over. He looked at his sister first.

"I decoded this on the island, the third night," he said. "I've carried it ten days. I'd give anything not to be right." He turned it.

No name. A drawing — the ledger's keyholder entry rendered as Joel's hand had received it: an emblem, the access-seal of the second Svartholmen key, the founder's counterpart key, drawn precise as an engraving.

A bird, breast bowed to its own wound, feeding three young from it.

A pelican in her piety.

The quay noise died around Vera in a widening ring, but she barely registered it, because she was falling through a fortnight in perfect order: a signet ring turning a coffee cup in lamplight, fine Latin, *whatever your brother left you, keep it on your person* — the warmth, the aquavit, the photograph he'd explained so reasonably, *grief always consults an expert*, the join she had found and let an old raven talk her out of — *every fact fits a guardian as well as it fits a wolf* — and Kessler's voice, kindly, by the fire: *trust the oath, not the man.*

He had even given her the rule that condemned him. Truth in bulk. Lies only at the join.

"Aron Kessler," she said, and her voice came out of her like something prised from ice. "The Society's great diplomat. The man who argued for initiating the founder." She looked up at Ruben, who had gone the color of his island's granite. "The man who has known every move we — Ruben. *Ruben.* He knew Edvard had the shelfmark. He knew I'd go to the boathouse, he met Joel, he knows about *you* —"

"He knows everything," Ruben said, "except one thing." The old man's voice had gone down to bedrock. "He doesn't know that *we* know. That folio hasn't left this harbor. The moment he learns it has —"

Vera's phone — the ordinary, other-world phone in her ordinary pocket — buzzed.

Afterwards she would understand the timing was not magic; it was simply that a man with EKO does not need magic, he needs only enough harvested probability to know when a household wakes. But standing on the quay in two worlds at once, it felt like the wolf clearing its throat in the room.

No number, as before. The message read:

*My dear Fröken Lind. I have been trying to reach our friend Dr. Ask for three days, and I am sick at heart — an old man, in this weather. Happily, I have found someone who knows the archipelago even better than he does, and she is keeping me company until he calls on us. Shall we say my house, tomorrow evening, all of us together? Do bring your brother's things. — A.K.*

And then, after three seconds, a second message. A photograph.

Sigrid's storm lantern, lit, standing on a desk Vera recognized, beside a chessboard, in the warm lamplight of the house below the castle.
