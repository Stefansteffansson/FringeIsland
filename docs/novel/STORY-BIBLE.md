# THE SHIMMER (working title)

A puzzle-driven conspiracy thriller, ~100 pages (~28,000 words), set in
present-day Stockholm and the FringeIsland universe, told in the eyes of a
person joining the community.

**Form:** Da Vinci Code shape, scaled — Prologue + 36 short cliffhanger
chapters (~650–800 words each) + Epilogue. Close third person, multi-POV.

---

## 1. The five pillars

**Central mystery.** Manuscript conservator Vera Lind's estranged brother
Joel — an early FIM — drowns off a Stockholm ferry. Case closed: suicide.
Then a padded envelope arrives: a small glass marble with a spark inside
(an anchor-seed), a hand-drawn map of a city that doesn't exist, and an
invite code to FringeIsland. Inside the platform, Joel's Whisp's lantern
is still burning — which everyone says is impossible. Why did Joel die,
why does his inner voice live on, and what trail did he build for the one
person who shares his childhood cipher?

**Protagonist.** Vera Lind, 34, paper-and-parchment conservator at the
Royal Library (Kungliga biblioteket), Stockholm. Patient hands, stalled
life, allergic to her own three questions. Joins FringeIsland as a Shadow
to follow her brother's trail — and is forced, step by step, to actually
answer Who am I? What do I want? How do I get there?

**Antagonist.** PELIKAN — the operating name of Professor Aron Kessler,
Uppsala historian of mysticism, ranking keeper of a 377-year-old society
and its secret traitor. He has sold the society's guarded practice to a
private contractor and built EKO: a persuasion engine trained on harvested
inner dialogue, run inside a mirrored, hostile copy of the Fringe (canon:
place 3). Field instrument: a fixer known as the Ferryman.

**Hidden secret.** The Whisp has an ancestry. In 1649, among the war loot
shipped from Prague with the Devil's Bible, came a slim companion volume —
the *Speculum Vocis*, "Mirror of the Voice": a medieval dialogic practice
for meeting one's inner voice as a companion. A society founded by Queen
Christina's librarians has guarded it ever since, under one oath: *the
voice may only ever be a mirror, never a hand.* A 1970s state project
(SPEGEL, on the island of Svartholmen) tried to weaponize the practice and
was buried. FringeIsland's founder built the Whisp as the Speculum's
ethical heir — graduation, the cord, the inviolable glass-ball home are
each an answer to SPEGEL's sins. Kessler sold SPEGEL's corpse, and the
oath, to the highest bidder. Joel found the breach — and hid the proof
(EKO's buyer ledger) where no subpoena or hacker can reach: inside his own
glass ball home, which only he can open. Which means Joel is alive.

**Stakes.** If EKO ships, manipulation stops looking like advertising and
starts sounding like your own inner voice — the end of inner privacy, at
civilizational scale. If the secret is exposed badly, it destroys the
society, the platform, and public trust in the one tradition that knew how
to do this safely. The novel's spine: a platform built to set people free
versus a machine built to capture them.

## 3. POV rotation

Vera (primary, ~70%) · the Ferryman (menace + eventual mirror of the
theme) · PELIKAN (scope of the threat, two or three chapters only).
Dan Brown rule: the reader is always slightly ahead on danger, always
slightly behind on truth.

## 4. Canon-conformance register (mechanics the novel must obey)

- **Shadow status** — anonymous auth, ephemeral data. Vera starts as a
  Shadow; the 24h data-expiry of her Shadow session forces the sign-up
  decision (ch 9–10).
- **The Shimmer** — membrane between Ordinary World and Fringe; every
  crossing rendered cinematically.
- **The Whisp** — each FIM's inner dialogue made companion AND their
  avatar in the FringeIsland world. EKO's harvested Whisps are *cordless*
  — the visible wrongness of the antagonist's work.
- **FIM–Whisp cord** — cannot be severed (or faked) without consent.
  This is the climax mechanic: consent can't be forged, so EKO can
  imitate voices but never *be* one.
- **Three Worlds** — Ordinary World / Shimmer / Fringe.
- **Fringe places 2 & 3** — co-located; place 2 warm/flourishing, place 3
  hostile/anti-flourishing. EKO trains in place 3 — the anti-village.
- **FringeIsland village** — safe harbor in the Beyond of place 2.
- **Glass ball home** — inviolable private home. Nobody, ever, but the
  FIM. The whole plot pivots on this: the ledger inside Joel's ball
  proves Joel lives.
- **Anchor-seeds** — budded from the home ball. Joel's five seeds, bound
  to real Stockholm locations, ARE the puzzle chain.
- **Graduation over retention** — thematic counter-image to EKO.

Rule: if a scene needs a mechanic the discovery doesn't support, the
scene changes, not the mechanic.

## 5. Atmosphere & inspiration rules

- **Stålenhag register:** mundane Nordic landscapes with one intruding
  strangeness — rust, antenna arrays, a humming bunker under an
  archipelago islet. Svartholmen (invented) is the set piece.
- **Åkerman register:** a meticulously complete invented place that feels
  *remembered* — rain-wet streets, neon almost-right, timetables for
  trains that never come. Used twice: Joel's hand-drawn map of the
  non-existent city "Gråhamn" (the puzzle cipher), and place 3 as the
  city you half-remember.
- **Inspiration, not depiction:** no real living persons as characters;
  Stålenhag and Åkerman are mood boards only; no borrowing of Den
  Svagiska Unionen, Rocco Rivarossi, or any of their content. The Codex
  Gigas appears as the real historical artifact it is; all marginalia,
  the Speculum Vocis, and the society are invented.
- **Season:** late October–November Stockholm. Early dark, rain, neon.
- **Real history used:** the 1648 Swedish sack of Prague; the Codex Gigas
  arriving in Stockholm 1649; Queen Christina's librarians; the Royal
  Library and its vaults (procedures fictionalized).

## 6. Style rules

- Chapters 650–800 words. Every chapter ends on a question, a reversal,
  or a closing door.
- Clear, fast, cinematic prose; intellect carried by puzzles and history,
  never by lecture. One idea per chapter.
- Show, don't explain; mechanics surface through Vera's newcomer eyes.
- Act structure = the founding questions: Act I *Who am I?* (1–12),
  Act II *What do I want?* (13–26), Act III *How do I get there?* (27–36).

## 7. Chapter outline

**PROLOGUE** — Rain, night, Söder Mälarstrand. Joel Lind flees, posts a
padded envelope, boards the Djurgården ferry, speaks one last sentence to
his Whisp — and goes into the black water. The Ferryman reports "done,"
but registers what he didn't hear: a splash.

### Act I — Who am I? (1–12)

1. Ten days later. Vera in the conservation lab; police close the case.
   The envelope: marble, impossible map, invite code. The marble glows at
   her touch.
2. Ferryman POV. PELIKAN's orders: the sister has a seed. Retrieve all
   of them. EKO glimpsed obliquely.
3. Vera installs the Gimbal as a Shadow. First Shimmer crossing. Her
   Whisp speaks — in her own cadence. "Who are you?"
4. The village: Joel's lantern still burning at the harbor — "that's
   impossible." Corvus warns her off. Her apartment has been searched.
5. The Gråhamn map is the childhood street-cipher. First seed wakes at
   Mosebacke: Joel's voice + an image of the Devil's Bible. She's
   being followed.
6. Royal Library, after hours. Skoog and the 18th-c. marginalia: a lost
   twin volume, *speculum vocis*, vanished from the catalogues. A name:
   Kessler, Uppsala. The alarm trips.
7. Chase through KB; escape by tram crowd. PELIKAN, watching: *she's
   running the route Joel built. Let her run.*
8. Uppsala. Kessler's history: the 1649 society, the oath — mirror,
   never a hand. And: the founder was initiated; the Whisp IS the
   Speculum, built in software.
9. Second seed, Skeppsholmen: "They built a third place. Don't trust the
   cord you didn't tie." A 1970s photo: Project SPEGEL. Then the Shadow
   expiry warning: 24 hours.
10. The decision chapter. Cord, consent, the glass ball home. Vera signs.
    Her home forms — and beyond the village lights, a second skyline
    flickers where no skyline should be.
11. Skoog is attacked for the shelfmark slip. Grief, guilt — and now the
    police want her.
12. Kessler summons her: the society will help. The reader sees the
    Ferryman's new orders — signed PELIKAN, sent from Uppsala.

### Act II — What do I want? (13–26)

13. Kessler's house, night: Prague 1648, SPEGEL 1972 ("the mirror talked
    back"), the contractor that bought its corpse — now inside the walls.
14. Into the Fringe with Corvus: Joel's sealed home; the seed points
    home but the third seed is in the Ordinary World. First cordless
    Whisp — hollow-voiced, wrong.
15. Gamla Stan, the German Church alley: Joel's technical fragment — EKO,
    exfiltration through the Shimmer, place 3 as training ground. CCTV;
    converging footsteps.
16. Chase through Gamla Stan. Corvus in the flesh: Dr. Ruben Ask, SPEGEL
    survivor. Escape on an archipelago ferry.
17. PELIKAN POV: the EKO demo — a market panic seeded through a thousand
    inner voices. He needs Joel's ball opened; the sister is bait to
    prove Joel lives.
18. Ruben's story aboard the ferry: Svartholmen, what the mirror said
    back, and why every FringeIsland mechanic is an answer to SPEGEL.
19. Fourth seed, an archipelago boathouse of maps: coordinates complete —
    Svartholmen. And: "One of the mirror-keepers is turned."
20. Night landing on Svartholmen. Rust, antennas, the humming bunker, the
    first server hall. Joel — alive. Then floodlights.
21. The island assault. Joel's confession on the run: the staged
    drowning, the ledger, the mole. They flee into the deep level — and a
    founder-era hardline crossing.
22. Place 3 traversal: the anti-village, the city you half-remember,
    harvested Whisps wearing borrowed faces. Toward the seam.
23. EKO tests Vera through imitations — Joel's voice, her mother's. The
    cord holds: consent can't be faked. Sanctuary in the village.
24. Joel opens his glass ball. The ledger: buyers — and a society sigil
    Vera has seen on a signet ring. Kessler. Who already knows; he has
    Ruben.
25. PELIKAN's parley: his creed — mirrors are for the weak; EKO is the
    voice of order. The offer: the ledger for Ruben.
26. The exchange at Skeppsbron, ambushed; the ledger device seized. Joel
    smiles: it isn't data, it's a seed — it opens only at a place. The
    place is the Royal Library. The Speculum never left.

### Act III — How do I get there? (27–36)

27. Endgame setup: Skoog's shelfmark — the Speculum bound *inside*
    another book, hidden in plain sight near the Devil's Bible. PELIKAN
    holds the same trail.
28. Vera's legitimate night access; the vault; the volume found. Its
    final leaf is a map: the practice ends by drawing your inner city.
29. The society's buried failsafe: *vänd spegeln* — turn the mirror. A
    protocol to reflect a steering hand back to its source. Applied to
    Whisps: every EKO nudge, shown and named, to every FIM at once.
30. PELIKAN and the Ferryman take the floor. The split: Joel to the
    Fringe to plant the turn; Vera and the Speculum into the stacks.
31. Intercut: the elder FIMs propagate the mirror-turn through the
    village; the seam to place 3 brightens; Vera cornered among codices.
32. The turn fires. Across the platform, every Whisp surfaces EKO's hand.
    A manipulation engine that is *seen* is dead. Place 3 goes dark,
    district by district.
33. The Ferryman reads his own EKO file — his orders were half synthetic.
    He walks. PELIKAN's last gambit: fire among the manuscripts.
34. The vault fire. Conservator's payoff: Vera knows exactly how books
    burn. PELIKAN taken on the steps at dawn. In custody, his own Whisp
    speaks — the one thing EKO never touched.
35. Aftermath: ledger to prosecutors and journalists; the Speculum
    catalogued at last, public; Skoog honored; Joel chooses the village
    for now.
36. Vera answers her three questions in miniature, buds her first
    anchor-seed, binds it to the lab bench where it began. The platform's
    promise: one day it will let her go.

**EPILOGUE** — Months later. A new Shadow crosses the Shimmer; at the
harbor, two lanterns burn where one used to. And somewhere in place 3,
one district still has its lights on — and someone is selling tickets.
